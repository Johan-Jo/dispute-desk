## executiveSummary
  [C10,C1] Blume's position is that the cardholder's claim that the merchandise was not received (Visa 13.1) is answered by the carrier's delivery record.
  [C1,C2] The claim fails in full because the carrier recorded as delivered the one shipment that held the entire order.
## fulfillmentArgument
  [C10,C1] A non-receipt claim turns on whether the carrier delivered, and here the carrier's own record, not the merchant's, reports the delivery the cardholder denies.
  [C4,C5,C1] The merchant's fulfilment entry on the day of purchase began the shipment; the carrier's delivery event completed it.
  [C3,C1] 
The issuer does not need to take the merchant's word, because the tracking record behind the link on the shipment card is published by the carrier and shows the delivery status directly.
## lineItemsArgument
  [C1,C2] The delivery covers the whole order, not a portion of it.
  [C2] The merchant's fulfilment record, checked line by line against the order, places every purchased item in the quantity ordered in that single tracked shipment, leaving no second or partial shipment for the claim to rest on.
  [C9,C2,C1] Because the order total equals the full disputed amount, the delivered shipment accounts for the entire sum in dispute.
## chronologyArgument
  [C7,C1,C8,C6,C10] After a shipping confirmation was sent to the email address on the order, the carrier recorded the delivery and a delivery notification was sent to the email address on the order that same day; the non-receipt claim was raised 75 days later.
## conclusion
  [C1,C2,C9] The carrier's delivery event, tied by the fulfilment mapping to every line of the order, leaves nothing the cardholder paid for outside the evidence.
  [C10,C1,C2,C9] The non-receipt claim is therefore answered in full, and reversal follows.
