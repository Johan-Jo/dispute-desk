# Dispute intel from the order timeline: what happened, outside DisputeDesk

**Status:** rev 2, 2026-09-29. Research complete (read-only, prod). Critic round 1: REVISE (12 findings,
all addressed below; see §10). No implementation.
**Origin:** Mein Maison #99445, an inquiry that showed "lost". The Shopify order timeline showed the
real story: the merchant's support app, then a staff member, refunded the customer ("wrong goods
delivered"), and Shopify closed the inquiry as refunded. None of that was visible in DisputeDesk.
**Related, and how this plan relates:** see §6 (one model, existing modules extended, not duplicated).

---

## 0. Two facts that shape everything

1. **Shopify keeps order events for about 90 days.** The oldest event across all 692 surveyed orders
   is 2026-07-01, exactly 90 days before the survey; 59 of 160 June disputes have no events left.
   Whatever we do not store, we lose. The survey (2026-09-29) is the only copy of July's events:
   `C:\Users\johan\Cursor Portfolio\DisputeDesk-intel-seed\survey-2026-09-29.jsonl` (14 MB, sha256
   `95791ff2eafc5e52…`, contains personal data, never committed to git). **Step 0 imports it.**
2. **Shopify attributes nothing on response events.** `dispute_pending` and `dispute_inquiry_pending`
   carry no app and no user, including on the 92 disputes DisputeDesk itself saved. Who responded
   cannot be read from the timeline; only *that* a response went, and what the merchant did around it.

## 1. What we found

### 1.1 Method

Read-only probes against the Admin GraphQL API (2026-01) with each merchant's stored offline token,
and a survey of every dispute opened in the last 120 days: **692 disputes, 663 orders, 4 shops**
(Mein Maison 555, Blume Box 108, Cay Collective 28, Surasvenne 1). Per dispute: the order's newest
150 timeline events (no order had 100 or more), refunds, dispute evidence, customer, cart attributes,
journey, metafields. Scripts are copied next to the seed file. Nothing was written anywhere.

### 1.4 A sync bug found on the way (separate fix, not this plan)

Four Mein Maison disputes show **won** in our DB but are **chargebacks under review** in Shopify
(#94534, #94448, #99123, #92590). Each was reopened after a win or an inquiry close; our DB has not
updated them since 2026-08-29. Checked live 2026-09-29: all four have a response on file (merchant
submissions on 22 Sep for #94534/#94448), so no deadline is being missed today, but a reopened case
after a close is invisible to DisputeDesk. The response-cycle work (#861) handles
`under_review → needs_response`, not a reopen after a close. **Own fix, own PR, before this plan's P1.**

## 2. What this changes

1. **Outcome truth.** Each decided cycle gets a *cause*: `bank_decided`, `merchant_refunded`,
   `merchant_accepted`, `customer_closed_inquiry`, `resolved_in_favour`, or `unknown_events_expired`.
   Reporting, the decided view, the outcome email and the intelligence engine stop counting settlements
   as verdicts, and show the gap where events expired instead of hiding it.
2. **The refund record for the counsel argument.** Timestamps of `refund_created` (with actor) feed the
   stance's "credit already issued" argument, the one positive bank-facing use (P3).
3. **The merchant sees what they and their tools did during the dispute**: refunds, cancels, returns,
   notes, with who did it, on the dispute page and in the outcome email.
4. **Honest response facts.** "A response was sent through Shopify on …" with, where true, "right after
   the refund on …". Not who sent it (§0.2).
5. **Signals for later:** the other dispute tool, inquiry-refund patterns per merchant, merchant fraud
   tags.

## 3. Principles

- **P1 Record, then derive.** Store the raw event (jsonb) plus parsed columns; the classifier is
  versioned and re-runnable without a re-fetch.
- **P2 Never a new reason to stand down.** Nothing in this plan stops building or filing. The only
  reasons not to file stay those of `merchant-counsel-stance.plan.md` §1 (Shopify Protect coverage,
  the merchant's Concede, the minimal verified response). A refund is argued as credit already issued;
  a fraud cancel on a shipped order is still defended. Accepted and refunded-inquiry disputes are
  already closed by the status sync.
- **P3 Merchant's counsel.** Adverse intel (fraud tags, "wrong goods delivered", cancellations) informs
  the merchant and never reaches a bank artifact. Bank-facing use is limited to allow-listed, helpful,
  independently true facts (refund timestamps; the chronology's existing allow-list).
- **P4 Text is parsed where it must be, and pinned.** Some facts exist only in text (cancel reason,
  the two "closed" variants, the actor's name). Each text-derived field is listed (§4.3) and pinned by
  tests on real Shopify strings. Merchant surfaces render classified `I18nToken`s in all six locales;
  raw Shopify text appears only as a labelled verbatim quote.
- **P5 Personal data.** Customer names, emails, staff names, IPs. Covered by named redaction and
  retention paths (§4.6).
- **P6 One model.** This plan's derivations feed the existing modules (§6); nothing runs beside them.

## 4. Design

### 4.1 Storage

`order_timeline_events` — key `(shop_id, event_gid)` unique; `order_gid`, `occurred_at`, `typename`,
`action`, `app_title`, `attribute_to_app`, `attribute_to_user`, `raw jsonb` (the event as returned),
parsed: `actor_name`, `message_text`, `details_json`, `category`, `category_version`; `source`
(`live` | `survey_2026-09-29`), `first_seen_at`, `last_seen_at`, `deleted_at`. RLS enabled
(service role only). Keyed on the order, **not** the dispute: an order can carry several disputes and
several cycles; the link to a dispute and its cycle is made when reading, by time window and
`response_cycle`.

### 4.2 Collector

- **Step 0 (now):** import the survey seed with `source='survey_2026-09-29'`.
- **Retention rule:** a disputed order's timeline is fetched within one day of the dispute opening and
  on every lifecycle change, so nothing expires before we hold it.
- **Triggers:** `disputes/create` and `disputes/update` webhooks and the `refresh-open-disputes` cron
  (lifecycle events); `orders/updated` for an order with an open or recently decided dispute (refunds,
  notes, cancels). Upsert on `event_gid`; `last_seen_at` refresh; a comment that disappears is marked
  `deleted_at`, not removed.
- **Pre-dispute history:** orders older than ~90 days at dispute time have already lost their early
  events. Decision D4: capture events at order ingest for all orders (volume) or accept the gap.
- **S2 shop-wide feed:** used only after its filters are verified (Q2). Without an `action:` filter it
  means reading every order event of the shop (Mein Maison: ~17k orders / 100 days); measure first.
- **Cost:** the survey took ~3 s per order including 0.4 s spacing; per-dispute fetches are fine,
  bulk backfill runs throttle-aware in the job worker at backfill priority (≥500).

### 4.3 Classifier (`lib/disputes/timeline/classify.ts`, versioned)

Categories: `dispute_lifecycle`, `response_sent`, `refund_triggered_response` (a response event within
10 min after a `refund_success`), `refund` (created/success/failure; actor from `refund_created`),
`cancel`, `return`, `note`, `staff_comment`, `customer_email`, `fulfilment`, `fulfilment_request`,
`order_edit`, `payout`, `risk`, `other`.
**Text-derived fields (pinned by tests on real strings):** cancel reason (from `additionalContent`),
`customer_closed_inquiry` vs `resolved_in_favour` (message), `actor_name` (message), refund reason
(`additionalContent`). Unknown actions increment a counter surfaced on the Admin health page (owner:
maintainer; weekly), never dropped.

### 4.4 Derivations (`lib/disputes/timeline/derive.ts`), per dispute cycle

- `outcomeCause` (§2.1), from lifecycle events only.
- `responseEvents[]`: `{ at, via: 'disputedesk' (our save record) | 'through_shopify', afterRefund: bool }`.
  No app or person is ever named as the responder.
- `merchantActions[]`: refunds (amount, actor, reason), cancels (reason, actor), returns, notes.
- `refundBeforeFiling`: the timestamped refund record the counsel argument uses.

### 4.5 Readers

| Reader | Uses | Bank-facing? |
|---|---|---|
| Decided view, outcome email (`decidedResponse.ts`, `decidedView.ts`) | `outcomeCause`, merchant actions | No |
| Dispute page, new section "During this dispute" | merchant actions, response events | No |
| Intelligence engine | `outcomeCause` → `withdrawn` / adjudicated split | No |
| Counsel "credit already issued" | `refundBeforeFiling` | Yes, allow-listed |
| Letter chronology | may move onto the table later; the existing allow-list (`chronology.ts`) applies unchanged | Yes, allow-listed |
| Fix A (`responded_via_shopify`) | `responseEvents` as a corroborating signal only | No |

### 4.6 Personal data

- `customers/redact`: delete events of the redacted orders (`orders_to_redact`) — add the table to
  `app/api/webhooks/customers-redact/route.ts`; `shop/redact` and `retention-cleanup` likewise.
- Staff names and IPs: stored only in `raw`/`actor_name`; shown to the merchant's own users only;
  removed with the shop.
- Privacy and data-retention pages list the new category (all locales).

## 5. Phases

- **Step 0 — Preserve (this week, before July expires ~1 Oct).** Migration + seed import of the survey
  file. Nothing else.
- **P0 — Collect and classify.** Collector with the retention rule, classifier, backfill of what
  Shopify still holds, Admin-only view, drift counter, DB-vs-timeline status check (§1.4 class).
  Acceptance: §1.3 reproduces from seed + live data, per shop.
- **P1 — Outcome truth** in the decided view, outcome email, admin analytics, intelligence engine.
- **P2 — Merchant section "During this dispute"** (six locales), incl. response facts (§2.4).
- **P3 — Refund record into the counsel "credit already issued" argument.**
- **P4 — Wider sources (decisions):** `read_returns`, helpdesk connectors, the other dispute tool (Q1).

## 7. Decisions needed

- **D1** Show staff and app names to the merchant (their own staff and tools). Recommend yes.
- **D2** Free-text notes and refund reasons: merchant-visible only, never bank-facing. Recommend yes.
- **D3** A merchant refund on an inquiry before Shopify closes it (≈ 1 day on PayPal): treat as the
  merchant's instruction, like Concede, only if the merchant confirms in the app; otherwise keep
  defending. Recommend: no automatic hold.
- **D4** Capture timeline at order ingest for all orders (keeps pre-dispute history) or only for
  disputed orders (accept the gap). Needs a volume measurement first.
- **D5** Retention: same as orders. Recommend yes.

## 8. Acceptance tests

- T0 The seed imports all 692 disputes' events; row count and sha match.
- T1 Every action seen in the survey classifies; an unseen action increments the drift counter.
- T2 #99445 → `outcomeCause = merchant_refunded`; response event on 29 Sep is `refund_triggered_response`;
  no responder names Inbox Unity.
- T3 Per shop: 117 `merchant_refunded` (Mein Maison), 22 `customer_closed_inquiry`, 18 `resolved_in_favour`
  (Cay), 164 `bank_decided` lost, 6 `merchant_accepted`, 31 `unknown_events_expired`.
- T4 Nothing in this plan changes a build or filing decision (gate inventory test unchanged).
- T5 Only allow-listed fields reach a bank artifact (static test over writer and chronology inputs).
- T6 `customers/redact` and `shop/redact` remove the events; retention cleanup covers the table.
- T7 Text-derived fields parse the real strings in the fixtures (English; unknown text → `unknown`, never a guess).

## 9. Risks

- Retention: a stalled collector loses data permanently. Alert when an open dispute's timeline is older
  than 24 h.
- Shopify UI text changes: text-derived fields fall back to `unknown`, never a wrong value.
- The shop-wide feed may not filter as hoped (Q2); per-order fetch is the default.
